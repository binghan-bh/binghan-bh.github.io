Research

Decoding epigenetic mechanisms in colorectal cancer: from disease biology to therapeutic opportunities.

Over the past decade my work has followed one question across three layers of gene regulation: how tumors rewire the control of gene expression, and where that rewiring can be targeted. I started with RNA modification in intestinal stem cells, moved to chromatin regulation at the interface of the gut microbiome and tumor immunity, and have carried this into drug discovery, cancer vaccine studies and large-scale analysis of public data.

Main lines

2015 – 2020 · Zhejiang University · Yale University

RNA modification and intestinal stemness

I showed that the m6A reader YTHDF1 boosts translation of Wnt pathway components and so amplifies Wnt-driven stemness in the intestine and colorectal tumors. This established m6A reading as a control point in intestinal tumor initiation and led to collaborative work on YTHDF1 in DNA damage, metabolism and transcription.

  • EMBO Reports 2020, first author
  • Cancer Letters 2021, first-author review of m6A reader proteins in cancer
  • Science Advances 2025; Nature Communications 2023; Science Advances 2023, contributing author

2021 – 2026 · University of California, Los Angeles

Microbes, chromatin and the tumor immune environment

As a postdoctoral researcher I built Apc-mutant mouse models of Fusobacterium nucleatum-driven colorectal cancer and used them to study how a gut bacterium reshapes chromatin regulation in tumor cells and the immune cells around them. The work combined in vivo models with single-cell RNA-seq, single-cell ATAC-seq, ChIP-seq and mass cytometry.

  • Fusobacterium nucleatum: Methods and Protocols, first-author book chapter on the mouse model
  • Nature Communications 2024, KMT2D deficiency and the Fanconi anemia/BRCA pathway, contributing author

2021 – present · UCLA · industry collaboration · independent project

From mechanism to therapeutic opportunity

Alongside the mechanistic work I have led projects aimed directly at treatment, each built around in vivo models and readouts that predict response.

  • Small-molecule discovery. Screening a series of BMI1 inhibitor analogs through a tiered biomarker funnel, from cell-based assays to in vivo efficacy in mouse tumor models.
  • Cancer vaccines. Preclinical in vivo evaluation of a KRAS-targeted mRNA vaccine in collaboration with a biotechnology company.
  • Computational target assessment. An independent project using public CRISPR screens (DepMap) and published perturbation transcriptomes to ask what sets a cancer cell's dependence on the chromatin regulator KMT2D across tumor types.

Beyond cancer

Bone metabolism and other collaborations

A project on bone metabolism that started from a clinical observation and moved into mechanistic studies in mouse models, plus collaborative work on bone regeneration materials, pulmonary fibrosis, environmental toxicology and placental biology.
